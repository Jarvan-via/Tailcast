## Page-structure patterns that currently rank

Common high-performing commercial structure:

1. Exact platform + repricing H1.
2. Immediate trial/demo CTA.
3. Three-step connect/sync/activate explanation.
4. Feature cards for monitoring, rules, automation, margin boundaries, history.
5. Marketplace-specific section, although often shallow.
6. FAQ with exact query variants.
7. Cross-links to other platform/integration pages.

Common content weaknesses:

- template text with only the marketplace name replaced;
- unsupported “real-time,” “AI,” “best,” “#1,” and sales-uplift language;
- little explanation of shipping-inclusive price, site/channel differences, async confirmation, rate limits, or failure states;
- Buy Box language copied from Amazon even when the operator uses different ranking terminology;
- ERP and order-management sections that distract from repricing intent;
- weak distinction between price synchronization, price rules, competitor monitoring, and true competitive repricing.

## Autopricy differentiation to prove, not merely state

- Marketplace-specific competitor selection and price/shipping semantics.
- Hard minimum and maximum boundaries.
- Separate states for calculated, submitted, waiting for platform, confirmed, rejected, and failed.
- Multi-shop workflow without claiming ERP functions.
- Exact Offer identity rather than EAN-based guessing.
- Honest support matrix and operator assessment for Mirakl-derived marketplaces.
- Concrete screenshots and examples from the real product, with sensitive seller data removed.

## Breakthrough opportunities

1. **Mirakl repricer definition page:** explain why Mirakl is a platform family, why one integration cannot promise identical behavior, and how operator-specific price channels and competitor visibility change the workflow.
2. **Worten execution-state content:** competitors talk about “real time”; Autopricy can explain the full path from market input to platform confirmation.
3. **FNAC vs Darty behavior:** a useful, specific comparison of shared XML infrastructure and distinct competition logic is stronger than generic platform pages.
4. **OnBuy confirmation delay and raise-after-winning:** use actual workflow constraints rather than generic Buy Box claims.
5. **Cdiscount native tool vs external repricer:** explain when native floor pricing is enough and when package feedback, bulk rules, and multi-shop auditability matter.

## Backlink intelligence limitations

No Ahrefs, Semrush, Moz, Majestic, or Search Console link export was available in this phase. DR/DA and referring-domain counts are therefore deliberately absent. Observed third-party placements—Cdiscount partner, Mercado Libre partner, Chrome Web Store, review platforms—are evidence of channel types worth evaluating later, not proof that the link itself drives ranking.
